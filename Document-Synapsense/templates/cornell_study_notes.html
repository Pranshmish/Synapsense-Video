Kinematics & Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) Formulations

Subject: Robotics Engineering • Unit: Forward & Inverse Kinematics
Core Principle
Why DH frames are standard for serial manipulators.
Parameters
$\theta_i, d_i, a_i, \alpha_i$ definitions.
Singularities
$\det(J(\theta)) = 0$ geometric locking.

1. Forward Kinematic Transform

The position and orientation of the end-effector with respect to the base frame is determined by successive homogenous transformation matrices:

$$^{i-1}T_i = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\ \sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\ 0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Analytical vs Numerical Inverse Kinematics

For embedded microcontrollers (ESP32 / ARM Cortex-M), analytical closed-form solvers are preferred over iterative Jacobian inversion because they run in $O(1)$ constant time ($\le 15\text{ ms}$) without convergence divergence.

Summary & Key Takeaway

Analytical kinematics eliminate unbounded iteration loops in RTOS tasks. Pair DH joint matrices with cubic Hermite splines to suppress acceleration spikes and gear train stress.
